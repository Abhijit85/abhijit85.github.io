Research

Most enterprise AI systems depend on data and tools that other teams or organizations own. My papers ask what agents can share in that setting without moving raw data, how they find the right source, and how they check what comes back. I came to the PhD after fifteen years of building data platforms.

Retrieval and tool routing across distributed sources

Where should an agent look, and what can agents share?

Federated retrieval is where this started. Our mapping study in Findings of EMNLP 2025 covers the work from 2020 to 2025 that combines federated learning with retrieval-augmented generation.

SYNAPSE and Typed Federated Artifacts carry the idea over to tool routing. Agents share what they have learned about which tool suits which request, packaged as schema-checked objects instead of model weights. That lets agents running different frozen LLMs use the same knowledge. On StableToolBench the federated setup was within 1.1 points of a centralized baseline.

Even inside one database, the right table can be hard to find. JoinGR follows foreign-key joins to reach tables a question never mentions, and on the BEAVER enterprise benchmark it raised perfect recall@15 from 0.339 to 0.645. In DeALOG (EMNLP 2026), specialist agents for text, tables and images coordinate through a shared log, with no central controller.

At MongoDBMy AWS re:Invent 2026 session is about the production version of this problem: agent state and memory on Atlas with Amazon Bedrock.

Checking model output

How does a system check its own answer, and when should a person take over?

OSCAR (COLM 2026) looks for hallucinations inside diffusion language models. It runs several denoising chains in parallel, treats the positions where they disagree as suspect, and rewrites those positions using retrieved evidence.

Evaluation needs the same scrutiny. For CaRE we re-ran seven remasking strategies for masked diffusion models on twelve models under matched compute. Sampling temperature explained most of the variation (η² = 0.91). Once compute was matched, the strategy rankings reversed.

EviSearch keeps a person in the loop. It links every value it pulls from a clinical trial paper to its source and sends uncertain cells to a reviewer. On its own it was 91.7% accurate; after a reviewer checked 15.6% of the cells, accuracy reached 95.2%.

My current work is on selective prediction for knowledge graph completion: when a model can certify a prediction, and when it should abstain.

Structured knowledge

How much model does a knowledge graph need?

For link prediction, less than recent work assumes. RelatE is a real-valued phase-modulus embedding that matches or beats complex-valued and deep models on standard benchmarks (MRR 0.521 on YAGO3-10). It also trains 24% faster than RotatE and uses 22% less GPU memory.

JobMatchAI (ACL 2026 System Demonstrations) uses knowledge graphs and semantic search to match candidates to jobs, and explains each match.

VentureJobMatchAI is also a company I co-founded out of CoRAL Lab, with Mayank Vyas and Vivek Gupta.

Running ML in production

Why do models stall between prototype and production?

The MLOps mapping study (CSCE 2024) catalogs what stops ML projects in the data, model-building and deployment pipelines, and recommends a tool-agnostic fix for each. TimeGate (CATS workshop, ICML 2026) adds time-boxed promotion gates for models retrained continually on a fixed compute budget.
